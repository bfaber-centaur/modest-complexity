Purpose: exploratory science, checked for correctness and ready for human review. We have retired simplicity as the organizing hypothesis. The question is: between the post-filter accuracy peak and collapse, what changes in the computation, and which changes cause the loss of correct answers?

Starting evidence (verify against the repo): at p=31, width 128, a single hidden ReLU layer, full-batch AdamW, lr .001, 299 training pairs, the dominant-frequency weight edit lifts three failed models from about 0% to 43–58% held-out accuracy. Further training raises this to 74–87% within roughly 100 updates, then original decay=1 brings accuracy near chance by 5k. With decay=0, accuracy remains 56–62% at 20k, though it declines from its peak. A rank trace remains after collapse; the collapsed network is not the original network. The early rise survives a smaller learning rate and a gradient-descent probe. These are local observations, not evidence of a universal mechanism.

Competing explanations: useful components shrink/disappear; competing contributions overwhelm them; feature/readout alignment or ReLU gating changes; combinations or an alternative you identify. Do not force a winner.

Important limitations: our filter changes weights and ReLU gates. It may supply useful structure rather than expose an untouched subnetwork. Random-feature controls showed PARTIAL gains (14–30%), not gains matching trained filtered models. Raw logit growth is not increasing relative preference. AdamW decay is decoupled: do not silently interpret it as ordinary gradient descent on cross-entropy plus an L2 penalty. Weight norm alone is not function complexity.
