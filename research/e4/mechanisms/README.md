## The decompositions: object, basis rule, reconstruction error

| measure | object | basis rule | reconstruction (max over 637 states) |
|---|---|---|---|
| three channels | logits on the 483 common pairs | each unit's readout row split, along the label axis, into its update-0 dominant input frequency k_j (fixed and label-free; the frequency the filter kept) and the rest; μ = mean hidden activation over the 299 training pairs | label offset μW + reference (h − μ)W_ref + off-reference (h − μ)W_off = label-centred logits; relative error 1.6e-15 |
| weight parts | U, V (token axis) and W (label axis) | the same k_j; DC separate | squared norms add to ‖·‖² (Parseval); error ≤ 1e-15 |
| a+b-mode alignment and coherence | logit table over all 961 pairs (training pairs included) | per unit, the 2D DFT of its activation at (k_j, k_j) times the conjugate DFT of its readout at k_j | the units' coefficients sum to the table's 3D DFT at (k, k, −k) for every k; relative error ≤ 1e-15 |

These Fourier splits are descriptive and task-informed. The split is on the readout side only: the reference channel still carries whatever the off-reference input weights and changed gates do to h. As a control, the same split gives A, the unedited memorizer, a reference-channel accuracy of 0.16–0.27. Tests check the identities on a random network, the signs and cancellation of the alignment on constructed ideal networks, and that each filtered start's readout lies entirely at its reference frequencies.

## Observations (ranges over the three sources; B unless stated)

**Behaviour.**
- At wd 1, accuracy on the common pairs goes 0.74–0.86 at update 100 → 0.62–0.74 at 300 → 0.22–0.27 at 1,000 → 0.06–0.09 at 2,000 → 0.02–0.03 at 5,000.
- P(a+b over S) goes 0.96–0.98 at 100 → 0.56–0.60 at 2,000 → 0.40–0.44 at 5,000.
- At wd 0, accuracy is 0.66–0.78 at 2,000 and 0.63–0.74 at 5,000.

**Label offset (E4): not involved in the decline.**
- From t_fit onward, removing μW changes accuracy by at most 0.04, and its mean margins are at most 0.02 logits.
- *At update 0 it matters:* removing it lifts accuracy from 0.43/0.54/0.58 to 0.61/0.69/0.81. Its effect has mostly vanished by t_fit, so part of the **rise** coincides with the offset being trained away. This is an observation about the rise, not tested further here.

**Exact margin split between update 100 and 2,000 (per source).** The mean margin of a+b over the operand-associated labels S falls by 2.54, 2.98 and 2.81 logits. Of that:
- the reference channel accounts for 1.10, 1.41 and 1.27 (it goes from 3.14–3.47 to 2.04–2.20);
- the off-reference channel accounts for 1.44, 1.57 and 1.54 (it goes from −0.29–−0.31 to −1.74–−1.88);
- the label offset accounts for under 0.01.

Over the unrelated labels N, the loss is smaller (1.08–1.43) and mostly in the reference channel (0.80–1.13); the off-reference channel is at most −0.30 there. So the new competing contribution is aimed at operand-associated labels.

**The same window at wd 0.** The reference channel's S-margin *grows* by 1.69–1.82, the off-reference channel's falls by 0.82–0.91, and the total rises by 0.77–0.95. At 2,000:
- the reference channel's S-margin differs between wd 1 and wd 0 by 3.5–3.8 logits;
- the off-reference channel's differs by only 0.6–0.7.

**The reference channel alone** has accuracy:
- at wd 1: 0.79–0.91 at 100, 0.56–0.62 at 2,000, 0.47–0.50 at 5,000 and 0.49–0.52 at 20,000;
- at wd 0: 0.82–0.92 from 300 to 20,000;
- for A: 0.16–0.27.

The off-reference channel alone is at 0.00–0.004 everywhere.

**Weights (Frobenius norms of the parts).** At wd 1, between 100 and 2,000:
- U and V together at the reference frequencies hold: 28.7–29.8 → 28.1–29.0;
- W at the reference frequencies shrinks by about 13%: 23.4–24.0 → 20.4–20.9;
- the off-reference parts grow from about 4 to 16–17 (U, V) and from about 3 to 14–15 (W).

At wd 0, the reference parts grow (U, V 31–32 → 39–41; W 25–26 → 32) and the off-reference parts grow less (to 11–12 and 9). After 5,000 at wd 1, every part grows again (norm 45 → 57 at 20,000), and the reference channel's margins recover partly by 20,000 (S 3.1–3.2) while the off-reference channel's grow further (S −3.8 to −4.1). Accuracy stays at 0.01–0.03.

**Phase alignment and coherence at the a+b mode (E3, phase): not seen.** R and coherence stay at 0.98–0.995 in every run at every state. That is at or above A's values (0.98–0.99). The useful amplitude Σ Re A_j falls 15–21% at wd 1 between 100 and 2,000 and grows 54–60% at wd 0.

**Gates.** Relative to update 100, 17–18% of (pair, unit) gates have changed by 2,000 at wd 1, against 7–8% at wd 0 and 3% for A.

**Definition dependence.** Recomputing the reference and off-reference channels with each state's own dominant frequencies changes the channel margins by at most 0.02 up to 5,000 and by at most 0.08 at 20,000.

## Reading (provisional)

- Between the peak and the collapse at wd 1, two exact parts of the logits move against a+b, by similar amounts and mainly on the operand-associated labels. The reference-frequency readout part loses about 35–40% of its held-out margin over S. The rest of the readout builds a contribution against a+b of the same size, starting from near zero. Neither the shared label offset (Janati et al.'s mean term) nor phase decoherence at the a+b mode plays a visible part. So the answer is neither erased from one component nor masked by a class offset. It is **outvoted**: the reference channel alone still gets about half the common pairs right at 5,000–20,000, well above what the same projection gives A, while the full network gets almost none.
- **The useful component's loss is not mainly weight shrinkage.** Its input weights keep their norm at wd 1, and its readout shrinks by about 13%, while its margin falls by 35–40%. The remainder has to come through the hidden activations (gates changed, and off-reference input parts feeding through W_ref) or through readout direction. This decomposition cannot tell which. So E1 holds for the channel's output, not for its weights.
- **What decay changes, compared with wd 0, is mostly the reference part.** Without decay it grows and stays ahead; the competitor grows somewhat more slowly. With decay the reference part stalls and slowly shrinks, and the competitor overtakes it during the decline window (300–2,000). Which of these is necessary for the collapse is what the intervention below asks.

**Strongest alternative.** There may be a single integrated change, the network re-memorising the training pairs as a lookup over operand labels, which this split cuts into two channels by construction. The basis is task-informed. The reference channel is not an isolated circuit, since off-reference input weights feed into it through shared units. And the same projection manufactures 0.16–0.27 accuracy from a pure memorizer. On this reading "reference shrinks plus competitor grows" is one process seen through a Fourier lens, not two mechanisms.

**Not supported by this evidence:**
- that decay erases the answers by shrinking the useful Fourier weights (the reference input weights keep their norm at wd 1);
- that the collapsed network still "contains" an a+b algorithm that a readout could recover: no decodability test or readout refit was done, and the reference channel's accuracy is a projection, not a decoder;
- that the label offset masks the answers during the decline.

## Intervention: where decay acts (D14, D15; not audited)

![intervention](figures/intervention.png)

**Design.** It was written before any code or run ([INTERVENTION.md](INTERVENTION.md), `203dc54`) and approved in D14. The six claimed runs are `e4-me-filt-wd1-{xref,onlyref}-w128-i{30000,30001,30002}-a1`. Everything matches the wd 1 D7 replays except where the decoupled decay acts:
- **xref** decays each weight minus its reference part. The reference part is each unit's cosine and sine at its update-0 frequency k_j.
- **onlyref** decays only the reference part.
- Nothing is frozen.

**Code and runs.**
- The shared-code patch is PR #37, pinned at `8108d8b` (D15). Without its flags, a rerun of a D7 replay reproduces all 89 checkpoints and all 501 metric rows ([results/patch_check.json](results/patch_check.json)).
- The projection files are [intervention/](intervention/), with hashes in the plan. channels.py also checks that each file equals this analysis's update-0 reference.
- The runs were launched in the claimed queue order from a clean worktree at `23bb74a`. All six completed, with empty git status in every config, taking 55 s elapsed with 2 workers.
- The analysis ran from the clean commit `7049399` ([results/intervention.csv](results/intervention.csv), [intervention.py](intervention.py)).
- Lane compute is now about 2.7 of 20 minutes.

**Immediate effect.** There is none: update 0 is the start file in every arm, and all differences come from training.

**Pre-stated reading applied** (f = (acc − acc wd 1)/(acc wd 0 − acc wd 1) on the common pairs at 5,000, per source):

| arm | f at 2,000 | f at 5,000 | common accuracy at 5,000 | pre-stated outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xref (reference parts exempt) | 1.51, 1.39, 1.31 | 1.60, 1.53, 1.37 | 0.998, 1.000, 1.000 | f ≥ 0.7 in all three: decay acting on the reference parts is **necessary** for most of the collapse |
| onlyref (decay on reference parts only) | −0.11, −0.09, −0.14 | −0.04, −0.03, −0.04 | 0.000 in all three | f ≤ 0.3 in all three: decaying only the reference parts **suffices** for the collapse |

The wd 1 replay ends at 0.02–0.03 and the wd 0 replay at 0.63–0.74.

**Observations, xref (reference exempt).**
- Accuracy never falls below its update-300 value. It reaches 0.96–0.99 by 2,000 and at least 0.99 by 2,300–4,000. Own held-out splits are 0.99–1.00, and training accuracy is 1 throughout.
- The reference parts grow:
  - U and V together from 31–32 at update 100 to 49–51 at 5,000;
  - W from 25–26 to 38.
- The off-reference parts peak near update 650–850 (U, V 6.6–7.1; W 4.9–5.2) and then shrink (to 4.6–5.2 and 2.9–3.3). For comparison, the wd 1 replay reaches 21 and 18 by 5,000.
- At 5,000:
  - the off-reference channel's margin over S is −0.31 to −0.35, against −2.4 to −2.6 at wd 1;
  - the reference channel's is 10.2–10.7, against 1.9 at wd 1 and 6.5–7.0 at wd 0.
- Gates changed since update 100: 8.5–8.9%, about the wd 0 value. Alignment R stays at 0.99.

**Observations, onlyref (decay on reference parts only).**
- Accuracy falls below 0.10 at updates 700–800, against 1,550–1,950 for the wd 1 replay. It is 0.000 from 2,000 on.
- The reference parts shrink: U and V from 29–30 to 8, W from 23–24 to 3.3–3.6. The off-reference parts grow to 49–51 (U, V) and 45–46 (W).
- At 5,000:
  - P(a+b over S) is 0.08–0.10, below A's 0.14–0.18;
  - the reference channel alone is at 0.01–0.02;
  - 42% of gates have changed since update 100.

**Size of the decay, post hoc** ([results/decay_magnitude.csv](results/decay_magnitude.csv); not part of the pre-stated reading). The logged decay step norm at 5,000 compares as follows:

| run | decay step norm at 5,000 | common accuracy at 5,000 |
|---|---|---|
| xref | 5.7–6.3e-3 | 1.00 |
| uniform wd 0.1 (dynamics lane) | 5.5e-3 | 0.37–0.43 |
| onlyref | 8.5–9.1e-3 | 0.00 |
| uniform wd 0.3 | 1.6e-2 | 0.10–0.12 |
| wd 1 | 4.5e-2 | 0.02–0.03 |

Decay steps of about the same size give opposite outcomes depending on where they act. The xref arm also ends *above* wd 0, which removing decay alone could not explain. So the arms are not explained by how much decay is applied.

## Reading of the intervention (provisional)

- **What the collapse needs.** Within 5,000 updates from these starts, the collapse needs decay to act on the reference-frequency parts. Decaying those parts alone produces it, faster than uniform decay does.
- **What happens when the reference parts are spared.** Decay on everything else does more than fail to collapse the network. It holds the off-reference competitor small: that part grows to about 7 and then shrinks. Meanwhile the reference parts grow unopposed, and the network reaches 0.99–1.00 on the never-trained common pairs.
- **The first figure, read again.** The competitor grew at wd 1 *because* the reference parts were being decayed, not independently of it. With the reference spared, decay suppresses the competitor instead of making room for it. In first-figure terms, E1 (the useful component's loss) is necessary, and E2 (the competitor's growth) follows from it under this objective.
- **Caveat, stated in advance and still applying.** The parts are coupled through the loss. With the reference spared, the training pairs are fit about 30× harder by cross-entropy (2e-4 against 7e-3 at 5,000), which leaves less gradient to build the competitor. "Necessary" here means that decay on the reference parts matters; it does not mean that their shrinkage alone is the mechanism.

**Strongest alternative.** The exemption is **task-informed**. The projection names, for each unit, the frequency the filter kept, and the decay then prefers exactly those frequencies. That is a frequency-selective regulariser pointing at a Fourier solution, so the 1.00 may be largely built in by the intervention. On that view the result shows that such a solution is reachable from B's start and stable to 5,000 under this objective. It does not show what ordinary training "wants". The C1 random-feature starts (audit) through the same exemption would test this cheaply.

**Not supported by this evidence:**
- that the failed runs contained an algorithm the intervention recovered (C1 already shows the rise and collapse without them);
- that this network would stay at 1.00 beyond 5,000 updates;
- that uniform decay of any strength would behave like either arm;
- that the shrinkage of the reference weights is, by itself, the cause. Shrinkage and competitor growth are coupled through the loss, as stated.

## Decay switched off mid-decline: channels of the dynamics runs (analysis only; not audited)

The dynamics lane's six D13 runs, `e4-dy-filt-wd1to0-u{300,1000}-w128-i{30000,30001,30002}-a1`, resume the e3 wd 1 runs of B at update 300 or 1,000 and continue at wd 0 to 20,000. The coordinator approved this analysis after D15 as analysis only, in lane code, with no new runs. The question is the dynamics lane's: which channels and weight parts are lost between updates 300 and 1,000 at wd 1, and do they stay absent while the decay-off runs regrow to the wd 0 norm? No reading was stated beforehand, so everything below is descriptive. One derived quantity, the competitor ratio, was chosen after seeing the table and is marked post hoc.

![decay off](figures/decay_off.png)

**Checks.**
- `channels.py` reads the runs with `check_resume`. For each run it checks four things:
  - the config matches the plan (resume path and hash, wd 0, 20,000 steps, width, lr);
  - the resume file is the parent run's published checkpoint, verified against the parent's manifest and the plan hash;
  - the run's first checkpoint equals that file and replays its logged metrics;
  - its data equal the parent's.
- **Basis.** The reference frequencies and the update-100 gates are those of the parent run. This is the same object and the same basis rule as the first figure.
- **States.** The analysis adds 144 states: every published checkpoint, which for these runs is every checkpoint written. The maximum reconstruction error over all 1,315 states is 1.6e-15.
- **Nothing else changed.** The 1,171 earlier rows are unchanged; this was checked against the previous table. The intervention tables and the two earlier PNGs are byte-identical.
- **Basis dependence.** With each checkpoint's own dominant frequencies (the `*_cur` columns), the off-run margins are identical, and the wd 1 margins differ by at most 0.08.
- **Provenance.** The analysis ran from the clean commit `0107249`. Table: [results/decay_off.csv](results/decay_off.csv).

**Observations.** Ranges are over the three sources. "wd 0" is the same start file trained at wd 0 from update 0.

1. **What wd 1 loses between 300 and 1,000. Nothing is removed outright.**
   - **Margin over S.** It falls 1.08–1.27 logits. The reference channel accounts for −0.49 to −0.62, which is 17–20% of its value at 300. The off-reference channel accounts for −0.59 to −0.66, so its margin roughly doubles (from −0.59…−0.64 to −1.17…−1.30). The label offset contributes ≤ 0.002.
   - **Weights.** The reference U and V hold (−0.4% to −1.9%). W at the reference frequencies shrinks 4.6–6.8%. The off-reference parts grow by 65–70% (U, V) and 80–83% (W). W at DC roughly doubles (+94–104%).
   - **wd 0 over the same updates.** The reference parts grow 9–10% and the reference S-margin grows 0.70–0.77. The off-reference parts grow 39–44% and the off-reference S-margin falls 0.34–0.37.
   - **Alignment.** R stays ≥ 0.99 throughout.
2. **After the switch the reference parts regrow. They do not stay absent.** At 20,000, as a fraction of wd 0's value:

   | measure | off from 300 | off from 1,000 | wd 1 throughout |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | U and V at reference frequencies | 0.98 | 0.94–0.95 | 0.61–0.63 |
   | W at reference frequencies | 0.95–0.96 | 0.89 | 0.59–0.60 |
   | reference S-margin | 0.92–0.93 | 0.75–0.78 | 0.29–0.32 |

   - The reference channel alone scores 0.78–0.87 (off from 300) and 0.65–0.76 (off from 1,000), against 0.82–0.88 at wd 0 and 0.49–0.52 at wd 1.
   - The reference margin rises from the first checkpoint after the switch.
   - The total norm reaches 74.2–74.7 (wd 0: 75.5–75.9).
3. **The competitor stays, and ends above the wd 0 run's.**
   - **Weights at 20,000.** The off-reference parts of U and V are 1.05–1.10× wd 0's (off from 300) and 1.25–1.29× (off from 1,000). For W they are 1.06–1.08× and 1.27–1.31×.
   - **Margin at 20,000.** The off-reference S-margin is 1.15–1.16× and 1.47–1.54× wd 0's.
   - **Just after the switch,** for 200–300 updates, the competitor grows faster than with decay kept on:
     - at 500, the off run is at −0.88 to −0.96 against wd 1's −0.79 to −0.87 (off-reference S-margin);
     - at 1,300, the off run is at −1.61 to −1.78 against −1.36 to −1.51.

     This overlaps the dynamics lane's 50–200-update lag. Later the competitor grows more slowly than at wd 1, but faster than at wd 0.
   - **The off-reference channel's cost.** Adding the off-reference channel and offset to the reference channel costs 0.33–0.40 of common accuracy (off from 300) and 0.44–0.53 (off from 1,000), against 0.26–0.29 at wd 0 and 0.46–0.51 at wd 1.
   - **Gates** changed since update 100, at 20,000: 0.13 (off from 300), 0.16–0.17 (off from 1,000), 0.12 (wd 0) and 0.26–0.27 (wd 1).
4. **Post hoc: the competitor ratio −(off-reference S-margin)/(reference S-margin) carries over the switch.**
   - **Off from 300.** It is 0.20–0.21 at the switch and rises about 0.08 by update 1,000. It then drifts by 0.07–0.09 to 0.36–0.39 at 20,000, about as wd 0 drifts over the same updates (+0.11–0.13, to 0.29–0.31).
   - **Off from 1,000.** It is 0.49–0.54 at the switch and rises 0.04 by 1,300. It is 0.55–0.61 at 20,000.
   - **wd 1** reaches 1.19–1.35.
   - Accuracy at 20,000 has the same order in every source. Both are functions of the same logits, so this order is a summary, not a test.

**Reading (provisional).**
- **Answer to the dynamics lane's question.** Between 300 and 1,000 at wd 1, the reference channel stops growing and loses a fifth of its margin, and the off-reference competitor doubles its margin.
- **Decay off.** The reference parts regrow with the norm, to 89–98% of the wd 0 run's.
- **What is missing at 20,000.** Not the reference parts. What persists is the competitor built under decay: it ends larger than at wd 0, and it keeps roughly its proportion to the reference margin.
- **Why the answers stay lost.** On this reading, the lost answers do not come back because the competitor keeps pace with the regrowing reference channel. A missing reference computation is not the reason.

**Strongest alternative.** Nothing here is specific to what decay built. At wd 0 the competitor grows from any state: its S-margin grows about fivefold from 300 to 20,000 in the wd 0 run itself. The decay-off runs may simply be wd 0 training continued from a state with a larger competitor, with no force that would shrink it. This analysis cannot separate "held in place by what decay built" from "kept growing by the training loss from that state". Both predict these curves.

**Not supported by this evidence:**
- **That removing the competitor would restore the answers.** The reference-alone accuracy is a readout ablation, not a trained state.
- **That regrown reference parts are the same computation as before.** Norms, margins, R and coherence return, but unit identity was not tracked.
- **Why the competitor accelerates just after the switch.** Adam's second moment is a candidate and was not tested.
- **Anything beyond 20,000 updates, other switch points, or switching decay back on.**

**Compute.** This was analysis only, with no training. `channels.py` ran twice (a dry run and the final run, about 37 s each) and the plots took a few seconds.
