## The decompositions: object, basis rule, reconstruction error

| measure | object | basis rule | reconstruction (max over 637 states) |
|---|---|---|---|
| three channels | logits on the 483 common pairs | each unit's readout row split, along the label axis, into its update-0 dominant input frequency k_j (fixed and label-free; the frequency the filter kept) and the rest; μ = mean hidden activation over the 299 training pairs | label offset μW + reference (h − μ)W_ref + off-reference (h − μ)W_off = label-centred logits; relative error 1.6e-15 |
| weight parts | U, V (token axis) and W (label axis) | the same k_j; DC separate | squared norms add to ‖·‖² (Parseval); error ≤ 1e-15 |
| a+b-mode alignment and coherence | logit table over all 961 pairs (training pairs included) | per unit, the 2D DFT of its activation at (k_j, k_j) times the conjugate DFT of its readout at k_j | the units' coefficients sum to the table's 3D DFT at (k, k, −k) for every k; relative error ≤ 1e-15 |

These Fourier splits are descriptive and task-informed. The split is on the readout side only: the reference channel still carries whatever the off-reference input weights and changed gates do to h. As a control, the same split gives A, the unedited memorizer, a reference-channel accuracy of 0.16–0.27. Tests check the identities on a random network, the signs and cancellation of the alignment on constructed ideal networks, and that each filtered start's readout lies entirely at its reference frequencies.

## Observations (ranges over the three sources; B unless stated)

**Behaviour.**
- At wd 1, accuracy on the common pairs goes 0.74–0.86 at update 100 → 0.62–0.74 at 300 → 0.22–0.27 at 1,000 → 0.06–0.09 at 2,000 → 0.02–0.03 at 5,000.
- P(a+b over S) goes 0.96–0.98 at 100 → 0.56–0.60 at 2,000 → 0.40–0.44 at 5,000.
- At wd 0, accuracy is 0.66–0.78 at 2,000 and 0.63–0.74 at 5,000.

**Label offset (E4): not involved in the decline.**
- From t_fit onward, removing μW changes accuracy by at most 0.04, and its mean margins are at most 0.02 logits.
- *At update 0 it matters:* removing it lifts accuracy from 0.43/0.54/0.58 to 0.61/0.69/0.81. Its effect has mostly vanished by t_fit, so part of the **rise** coincides with the offset being trained away. This is an observation about the rise, not tested further here.

**Exact margin split between update 100 and 2,000 (per source).** The mean margin of a+b over the operand-associated labels S falls by 2.54, 2.98 and 2.81 logits. Of that:
- the reference channel accounts for 1.10, 1.41 and 1.27 (it goes from 3.14–3.47 to 2.04–2.20);
- the off-reference channel accounts for 1.44, 1.57 and 1.54 (it goes from −0.29–−0.31 to −1.74–−1.88);
- the label offset accounts for under 0.01.

Over the unrelated labels N, the loss is smaller (1.08–1.43) and mostly in the reference channel (0.80–1.13); the off-reference channel is at most −0.30 there. So the new competing contribution is aimed at operand-associated labels.

**The same window at wd 0.** The reference channel's S-margin *grows* by 1.69–1.82, the off-reference channel's falls by 0.82–0.91, and the total rises by 0.77–0.95. At 2,000:
- the reference channel's S-margin differs between wd 1 and wd 0 by 3.5–3.8 logits;
- the off-reference channel's differs by only 0.6–0.7.

**The reference channel alone** has accuracy:
- at wd 1: 0.79–0.91 at 100, 0.56–0.62 at 2,000, 0.47–0.50 at 5,000 and 0.49–0.52 at 20,000;
- at wd 0: 0.82–0.92 from 300 to 20,000;
- for A: 0.16–0.27.

The off-reference channel alone is at 0.00–0.004 everywhere.

**Weights (Frobenius norms of the parts).** At wd 1, between 100 and 2,000:
- U and V together at the reference frequencies hold: 28.7–29.8 → 28.1–29.0;
- W at the reference frequencies shrinks by about 13%: 23.4–24.0 → 20.4–20.9;
- the off-reference parts grow from about 4 to 16–17 (U, V) and from about 3 to 14–15 (W).

At wd 0, the reference parts grow (U, V 31–32 → 39–41; W 25–26 → 32) and the off-reference parts grow less (to 11–12 and 9). After 5,000 at wd 1, every part grows again (norm 45 → 57 at 20,000), and the reference channel's margins recover partly by 20,000 (S 3.1–3.2) while the off-reference channel's grow further (S −3.8 to −4.1). Accuracy stays at 0.01–0.03.

**Phase alignment and coherence at the a+b mode (E3, phase): not seen.** R and coherence stay at 0.98–0.995 in every run at every state. That is at or above A's values (0.98–0.99). The useful amplitude Σ Re A_j falls 15–21% at wd 1 between 100 and 2,000 and grows 54–60% at wd 0.

**Gates.** Relative to update 100, 17–18% of (pair, unit) gates have changed by 2,000 at wd 1, against 7–8% at wd 0 and 3% for A.

**Definition dependence.** Recomputing the reference and off-reference channels with each state's own dominant frequencies changes the channel margins by at most 0.02 up to 5,000 and by at most 0.08 at 20,000.

## Reading (provisional)

- Between the peak and the collapse at wd 1, two exact parts of the logits move against a+b, by similar amounts and mainly on the operand-associated labels. The reference-frequency readout part loses about 35–40% of its held-out margin over S. The rest of the readout builds a contribution against a+b of the same size, starting from near zero. Neither the shared label offset (Janati et al.'s mean term) nor phase decoherence at the a+b mode plays a visible part. So the answer is neither erased from one component nor masked by a class offset. It is **outvoted**: the reference channel alone still gets about half the common pairs right at 5,000–20,000, well above what the same projection gives A, while the full network gets almost none.
- **The useful component's loss is not mainly weight shrinkage.** Its input weights keep their norm at wd 1, and its readout shrinks by about 13%, while its margin falls by 35–40%. The remainder has to come through the hidden activations (gates changed, and off-reference input parts feeding through W_ref) or through readout direction. This decomposition cannot tell which. So E1 holds for the channel's output, not for its weights.
- **What decay changes, compared with wd 0, is mostly the reference part.** Without decay it grows and stays ahead; the competitor grows somewhat more slowly. With decay the reference part stalls and slowly shrinks, and the competitor overtakes it during the decline window (300–2,000). Which of these is necessary for the collapse is what the intervention below asks.

**Strongest alternative.** There may be a single integrated change, the network re-memorising the training pairs as a lookup over operand labels, which this split cuts into two channels by construction. The basis is task-informed. The reference channel is not an isolated circuit, since off-reference input weights feed into it through shared units. And the same projection manufactures 0.16–0.27 accuracy from a pure memorizer. On this reading "reference shrinks plus competitor grows" is one process seen through a Fourier lens, not two mechanisms.

**Not supported by this evidence:**
- that decay erases the answers by shrinking the useful Fourier weights (the reference input weights keep their norm at wd 1);
- that the collapsed network still "contains" an a+b algorithm that a readout could recover: no decodability test or readout refit was done, and the reference channel's accuracy is a projection, not a decoder;
- that the label offset masks the answers during the decline.

## Proposed intervention (sent to the coordinator; nothing claimed or run)

The proposal is to restrict where decay acts: B at wd 1 with decoupled decay **exempting** the reference parts (decay acts only on the off-reference and DC parts). The complementary arm decays **only** the reference parts. The design, IDs and pre-stated reading are in [INTERVENTION.md](INTERVENTION.md), written before any intervention code or run.
