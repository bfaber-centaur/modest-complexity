# e2 dynamics: interpretations

Read [README.md](README.md) and its figures first. Each entry is a candidate explanation, its strongest alternative, and what would separate them. Nothing here is audited.

## 1. The 50% runs outgrow the operand-label preference instead of removing it (packet 1)

Candidate: on the common pairs, the difference between the shares is a rise of the correct label relative to all wrong labels, which starts between 1,000 and 2,000 updates at 50% and does not happen at 30% (width 128). The preference for operand-associated labels over unrelated ones (S − N gap) is not removed when the 50% runs start answering correctly: it stays near 4 logits from 1,000 to 50,000 updates, and the correct label passes the strongest unrelated label first and the strongest operand-associated label about 4 logits later. On this reading the "beats unrelated labels, loses to operand labels" state that holds 57-71% of pairs at 5,000-10,000 updates is what a uniform rise must pass through given that offset, not a separate stage with its own cause. At 30% the margin over unrelated labels stays flat and the operand-label preference doubles, so the correct label falls further behind.

What fits less well: the D labels (seen with an operand only through the 50% share's extra pairs) sit about 2.5 logits above unrelated labels in the 50% runs, below the S labels' 4, so the preference scales with something (perhaps how many training pairs carry the association), and the margin over S is not a fixed offset from the margin over N pair by pair; only the medians move in parallel.

Alternative: the gap is bookkeeping of memorization, not a competitor. At 30% the training loss keeps falling to 50,000 (train cross-entropy at width 128 falls 3.9x from 25,000 to 50,000; audit Part 4), and memorizing a training pair (a, b') raises its label on every pair that shares operand a, so the gap grows with fit; at 50% the training pairs are fitted by the same solution that answers held-out pairs, so nothing pushes the gap. Then the gap would be a consequence of whether the generalizing solution is found, and only the correct-label rise would matter. Separating check, done ([margins_fit.png](figures/share/margins_fit.png)): the gap is not set by training fit alone, since at the same training cross-entropy (1e-4) it is about 7 at 30% and about 4 at 50%. That rules out the plainest version of the alternative (fitting to a given loss necessarily grows the gap). It does not decide cause and consequence: the 50% runs leave the 30% curve near a training cross-entropy of 0.1, the same point where their margin over N starts rising, so with these checkpoints neither change visibly comes first. Width at 30% adds a counterpoint: wider networks raise the correct label against unrelated labels (median margin from about −3 at width 16 to about 0 at width 512) while their gap grows to the same 8 logits, so a rising margin over N does not by itself stop the gap growing.

Strongest counterexample to look for: a run whose correct label rises against unrelated labels as fast as the 50% runs' while its gap grows like the 30% runs'. Cycle 2 (QUESTION.md Q2) can produce one.

## 2. A race between two graded quantities, with a threshold on top (packet 2)

Candidate: along this nested ladder, each added training pair per class does two things to the common pairs: the correct label rises faster above unrelated labels (N), and the preference for operand-associated labels (S over N) grows more slowly. Neither quantity switches; both change in steps that track the share, with little seed spread. A held-out pair is answered correctly only when the correct label clears the operand-associated labels, roughly when the margin over N exceeds the S − N gap, so two graded changes produce a steep change in accuracy between 40% and 50%. At 40% the correct label beats every unrelated label on about two thirds of the common pairs by 50,000 updates while the preference grows almost as fast, which is "partial relational structure" without answers that does not resolve within the horizon. Entry 1's "outgrow, not remove" is the 50% end of this: there the gap stops growing; at 45% it grows but more slowly than the margin over N; at 40% it nearly keeps pace.

What would weaken it: a run at some share whose accuracy jumps while both medians stay smooth would mean the medians miss the event. Pair by pair the ordering is at least consistent: nearly every pair that becomes correct had already beaten every label outside the run's own set.

Alternative 1 (identity, not amount): the added pairs follow one seeded order, and at 40% and 45% they are e1 calibration pairs. Each added group could matter for which operands and classes it covers, so "graded in share" may be "this particular sequence of groups". A second data seed at the same shares separates the two; it was not run in this round.

Alternative 2 (dilution): with more training pairs, each operand co-occurs with more labels (on the common pairs, 16.0 operand-associated labels on average at 30% and 22.8 at 50%), so whatever the network stores per operand is spread over more labels and the strongest one stands out less. The slower gap growth would then be a property of a memorizer given more pairs per operand, not part of finding the correct answer. This predicts that shuffled-label runs at the same nested shares show the same slowing of the preference with share, although they cannot answer held-out pairs. The 30% shuffled-label runs from e1 exist (`e1-st-shuf-w128-*`), but there are none at higher shares.

Best next question: at the same nested shares, does the operand-label preference of a shuffled-label network also stop growing as the share rises? If it does, the gap is dilution and the race has one causal side; if it keeps growing at every share, the slowing seen here belongs to the runs that find correct answers.
